Autumn

The hills have caught a slower fire,
in copper, gold, and burgundy,
and leaves drift down without desire,
as if they're falling just to see.

I walk the lane at half past four,
the light gone honey-thick and low,
and smoke from someone's chimney pours
its gray across the afterglow.

The pumpkins sit on wooden stairs,
the apples soften in the bowl,
I save the light, the smoky air,
a story folded in my soul.

I think of roads that stretch too far,
of places that I cannot see,
and let the thought be what they are,
just leaves that settle over me.

The evening wraps the world in rust,
the lamps come on in amber rows,
and I go home, as all things must,
to one warm cup, the show she chose.
